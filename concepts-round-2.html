Round 2 · simpler concepts

Three Fibonacci games with one rule each

The last batch asked players to learn a theorem before they could play. These don't. Each one has a single rule you can read in about four seconds, and none of them require knowing what Fibonacci is — you just pick it up by playing.

All three are playable in the phones below.

CONCEPT A

What Comes Next?

The numbers are on screen. Tap the one that follows.

The only rule

Add the last two numbers. Tap the answer.

The two numbers you need are always highlighted in gold with a + between them, so the rule teaches itself on the very first turn — a player who has never heard of Fibonacci just sees 3 + 5 and taps 8.

Difficulty comes free: the numbers grow, so by 144 + 233 you're doing real mental arithmetic under streak pressure. No timer, no lives — get it wrong and it just shows you the sum and your streak resets. Nothing to punish a beginner.

CONCEPT B

Bloom

Tap to grow a sunflower. That's it.

The only rule

Tap. The flower grows.

There is nothing to understand and nothing to lose. Each tap drops in the next batch of seeds — 1, then 1, then 2, 3, 5, 8, 13 — and every seed lands at the golden angle of 137.5° from the last one. Spiral arms appear on their own, because that's genuinely how a real sunflower packs its seeds.

The first few taps look like almost nothing. Then around tap 8 it visibly explodes, which is the entire emotional arc of exponential growth delivered without a single word of explanation. The Fibonacci part isn't taught — it's just true, and you can see it.

As a shipped game this becomes a collection: different flowers, different seed colours, a garden you fill up. The tap is the whole loop.

CONCEPT C

Catch

Numbers fall. Catch the right one.

The only rule

Slide the basket. Catch the number that equals the sum.

The sum you need is printed at the top of the screen — 5 + 3 = ? — so there's no hidden knowledge at all. It's addition you already know, with arcade pressure to make it a game instead of a worksheet.

Catch right and the sum rolls forward to the next pair, which is where Fibonacci sneaks in: the answer you just caught becomes part of the next question. Players notice the pattern after a few rounds and start reaching for the answer before reading it — that's the moment the sequence clicks.

Three lives. Catching a wrong number or letting the right one fall costs one.

Recommendation: Bloom

It's the only one of the six concepts so far where a player needs no instructions and cannot fail. There's one button. The Fibonacci sequence isn't a puzzle to solve, it's just what makes the flower look right — which is a much easier sell than "learn this sequence".

It's also the most distinctive. Number-tapping games and falling-object games both already exist by the hundred; a tap-to-grow sunflower where the maths is genuinely why it's beautiful is something people screenshot and send to a friend.

What Comes Next? is the fastest to build if you want something finished this week, and pairs well as a mini-game inside a bigger app. Catch is the most game-like of the three and the best fit if you want kids as the audience — but it needs the most tuning to feel good.
