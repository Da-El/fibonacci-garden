Four mobile games built on the Fibonacci sequence

Each concept uses a different mathematical property of the sequence as its core mechanic — not just Fibonacci numbers as decoration. All four prototypes below are playable right here. Tap into the phones.

CONCEPT 01

FibMerge

2048, but tiles only merge with their Fibonacci neighbour.

Swipe to slide tiles. Two tiles merge only if they're consecutive in the sequence — a 3 merges with a 5 to make 8, but never with another 3. (The one exception: 1 + 1 = 2, exactly as the sequence starts.)

That single rule change transforms the 2048 feel completely. You can't mash a direction and hope for pairs — you have to build a ladder, keeping ascending runs intact. It's noticeably harder and much more deliberate.

The math it teaches

The recurrence itself. Players internalise F(n−1) + F(n−2) = F(n) as muscle memory, because it's the only legal move. Also surfaces the ratio: each tile is ≈1.618× the one before it.

CONCEPT 02

Zeckendorf

Hit the target number using Fibonacci tiles — no two touching.

A target appears. Tap Fibonacci tiles to sum to it exactly. The catch: you may never use two consecutive Fibonacci numbers. Picking 8 instantly locks both 5 and 13.

This isn't an arbitrary restriction — it's Zeckendorf's theorem, which guarantees every positive integer has exactly one such representation. So every round has a unique solution, and a greedy strategy (always grab the largest tile that fits) always works. Players discover that algorithm on their own within about ten rounds.

The math it teaches

Zeckendorf's theorem and greedy decomposition — effectively counting in Fibonacci base. 88 = 55 + 21 + 8 + 3 + 1, and no other combination works.

CONCEPT 03

Golden Rhythm

A rhythm game where the beat gaps are the sequence.

Notes travel toward the hit line and you tap as they cross. But the spacing between them isn't a steady 4/4 — the gaps cycle through 1, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8 beat units, then restart. Each note is drawn at a size matching the gap that follows it, so you can read the pattern coming.

The result is a lurching, self-similar groove: two quick hits, a breath, a longer breath, a long pause, then a snap back to fast. Once it clicks it's extremely satisfying, and it's a rhythm nobody has in their muscle memory yet — which is the whole pitch.

The math it teaches

Fibonacci as proportion in time rather than quantity. Players feel the ratio between successive terms approaching φ — every gap is roughly 1.6× the one before it. This is real compositional technique (Bartók, Lutosławski used it).

CONCEPT 04

Rabbit Ranch

The original 1202 problem, as a cozy idle game.

Fibonacci introduced the sequence in Liber Abaci by asking how fast rabbits breed. The rules he set: a pair takes one month to mature, and every mature pair produces one new pair each month. Nothing dies.

That's the entire game loop. Advance the month and watch adults = adults + juveniles, juveniles = previous adults. The total is always the next Fibonacci number — the growth curve is the reward, and it goes exponential right when the pen starts feeling full.

The full version adds pressure Fibonacci left out: feed cost scales with population, so you're forced to cull or expand. Cozy on the surface, an exponential-growth squeeze underneath.

The math it teaches

Where the sequence came from, and why recursive growth is deceptive. A' = A + J, J' = A. Reaching 144 pairs takes 11 months — but 6 of those months you're under 10 rabbits.

Recommendation: ship Zeckendorf first, FibMerge second

Zeckendorf is the smallest complete game here — a weekend build, no art pipeline, no audio, no economy tuning — and it's the only one where the math is the fun rather than a skin over it. Perfect first release to test whether "Fibonacci game" finds an audience at all.

FibMerge is the one most likely to actually retain players, because the genre is proven and the Fibonacci constraint is a real, felt twist rather than a reskin. But it's entering a crowded market, so validate the audience with the cheap one first.

Rabbit Ranch has the best long-term revenue shape of the four — worth building if this becomes more than a side project. Golden Rhythm is the most original idea and the riskiest; it needs a composer before it needs a programmer.
